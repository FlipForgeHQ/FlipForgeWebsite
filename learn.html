01 · OWN THE PROBLEM

The number is precise. The evidence may not be.

False Confidence is the gap between how certain a sports-card price looks and how much the evidence actually deserves to support the decision.

01

Wrong parallel

Same player, year, set and grade can still be a different market when the parallel or variation changes.

Identity before valuation
02

Stale comp

A reported completed sale can still mislead when the market around the decision has materially moved.

Timing changes relevance
03

Thin evidence

One exact dollar amount is still one observation. Precision is not the same thing as support.

One sale is not certainty
04

Grade assumption

A PSA 10 headline can dominate the decision while realistic lower-grade outcomes erase the apparent edge.

Model more than best case
02 · BUSTED COMP

One recurring question: does this sale deserve to influence the decision?

Each case starts with a number that looks useful. The lesson is the evidence rule that determines whether it actually belongs.

CASE 01 · TARGETExact card, exact parallel, exact grade

The decision needs evidence from the same card identity and grade lane.

→
CASE 01 · WRONG PARALLEL$900 sale. $850 listing. Easy buy?

Not if the $900 record is a different parallel. Similar-looking cards cannot silently become the same market.

02

One-sale trap

One exact sale feels authoritative. It may be useful, but a single observation should not manufacture confidence the evidence base does not deserve.

Thin evidence stays thin
03

PSA 10 mirage

The premium looks huge. Model lower-grade probabilities, raw opportunity cost, grading cost and liquidity before calling the upside real.

Expected value over headline value
04

Stale market

The sale was reported. The timing is wrong. An older completed sale may no longer represent the market around today’s decision.

Reported does not always mean relevant
05

ASK ≠ EVIDENCE

A seller can ask anything. Active listings are useful context, but they are never substituted for governed completed-sale evidence.

Asking price is context
06

Scarcity story

Numbered does not automatically mean liquid. Scarcity needs market context when neighboring parallels compete for the same collector demand.

Scarce is not the same as liquid
03 · EVIDENCE RULES

The discipline is simple enough to remember.

FlipForge can be sophisticated underneath while the customer keeps a clear mental model.

01

Resolve the card first.

Identity before valuation. Year, set, card number, parallel, variation, grader and grade must belong to the target before price evidence gets a vote.

Card first
02

Not every sale is a comp.

Exact, context and excluded evidence stay distinct. A nearby card can be useful context without being allowed to support the exact value.

Separate evidence lanes
03

Uncertainty is information.

VERIFY is better than manufactured certainty. When evidence conflicts, the correct decision can be to stop and confirm what is still unresolved.

Preserve uncertainty
04 · 7 / 14 / 30 REVIEW

Do not trust a decision engine because it sounds confident. Measure how its calls age.

The original decision stays fixed. Later governed evidence tests what held, what failed and where confidence needs recalibration.

DAY 7

Early signal check

Review whether the original evidence and market conditions still support the saved reasoning.

DAY 14

Decision stability

Stress changes in supported value, liquidity, evidence strength and risk without rewriting history.

DAY 30

Outcome review

Compare the thesis with later evidence and preserve what held, what failed and what remains uncertain.

Evidence standard: No public accuracy percentage is authorized until governed outcome data supports it. No fabricated win rate and no hindsight rewrite.
