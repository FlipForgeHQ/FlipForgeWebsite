Decision Foundations

Five disciplines before the recommendation.

A card decision becomes more defensible when each question has a separate job.

01

Identity

Start with year, set, player, card number, parallel, variation, grader, and grade. If the target is wrong, every price comparison downstream can be wrong too.

Rule: resolve the card first
02

Evidence

A completed sale is useful only when it is relevant enough to the target. Exact matches, context records, and rejected mismatches should not be blended into one average.

Rule: not every sale is a comp
03

Economics

Asking price, supported value, taxes, shipping, selling fees, grading costs, and grade probabilities all change the setup. Best-case upside is not the same as expected value.

Rule: model the full cost
04

Risk + Confidence

Confidence should reflect the quality of the evidence, not the excitement of the opportunity. Liquidity and downside matter even when the apparent value gap looks attractive.

Rule: uncertainty is information
05

Decision Traceback

A recommendation is stronger when you can inspect what caused it, what supported it, what was excluded, and what could change it later.

Rule: preserve the why
06

Outcome Review

The decision is not finished when the screen says BUY CANDIDATE, WATCH, VERIFY, or PASS. Later evidence is how the original reasoning gets tested and calibrated.

Rule: measure after the decision
Bad Comp Anatomy

One wrong parallel can ruin the entire comparison.

Illustrative example: the prices may look close while the identity evidence says they belong to different markets.

Target card2018 Topps Chrome #150 Refractor · PSA 10

The decision needs evidence from the same card identity and grade lane.

→
Returned record2018 Topps Chrome #150 Base / Unstated · PSA 10

Same player, year, set, card number, and grade—but the parallel is unresolved. That record should not automatically support the Refractor value.

!
The correct response is not “average it anyway.”

Keep the record visible for traceback, exclude it from exact support, verify the parallel, and rerun the evidence review. This is why FlipForge treats identity as a gate.

Grade or Keep Raw

The PSA 10 price is not your grading profit.

A grading decision should compare multiple outcomes, not assume the best case.

A

Start with raw value

The raw card already has value. That value is the opportunity cost of grading and belongs in the calculation.

Do not treat raw as $0
B

Add all grading cost

Submission fee, shipping, insurance, turnaround time, selling fees, and other transaction costs can materially change the expected result.

Use all-in cost
C

Weight the outcomes

PSA 10, PSA 9, PSA 8 or lower, and no-grade scenarios should be assigned realistic probabilities that total 100%.

Expected value beats wishful value

7 / 14 / 30 Review

A decision engine should be judged after the decision.

The goal is calibration—not a marketing accuracy number invented before the outcome data exists.

DAY 7

Early signal check

Ask whether the original evidence and market conditions still support the saved reasoning.

DAY 14

Decision stability

Review changes in supported value, liquidity, evidence strength, and risk without rewriting history.

DAY 30

Outcome review

Compare the original thesis with later evidence and preserve what held, what failed, and what the model should learn.

Evidence standard: FlipForge should publish performance metrics only when governed outcome data supports them. No fabricated win rate, no invented 90% accuracy claim, and no “24/7 monitoring” promise beyond actual product capability.
